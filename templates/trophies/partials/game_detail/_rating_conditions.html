{% comment %}The recommendation split -- the one thing here that is a VERDICT rather than an average, and the only one that answers "should I". It sits in the hero band with the score and the hours because it is a headline fact, not a fourth quality axis. ALL THREE options, including the ones nobody picked. Showing only the recommend share reports "everybody said Do it" and "most said Do it, one said skip" as 100% against 83%, when the dissent is often the interesting half -- and a zero is a fact too: nobody calling a platinum a slog says something about the game. Figure over word, the same idiom as the quality tiles above: the percentage leads, the raw count is a quiet subscript, and the label says which answer it was. The N is stated once underneath at every sample size rather than hidden behind a floor -- 80% is honest at five ratings and misleading without them, and only the count tells the reader which they are looking at. The block is present but collapsed until someone has answered (every game until the recommendation backlog clears), so the live-update only ever sets values and never builds DOM.{% endcomment %}
