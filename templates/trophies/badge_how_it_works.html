The badge system

Platinum the set.
Earn the medallion.

Every badge is a themed set of PlayStation games: a franchise, a studio, a collection someone curated. Clear the set and the medallion is yours, with the series' own artwork on its face.

{% comment %} THE JOURNEY. Four beats, each with its own art, revealed as you scroll. Wording checked against `badge_engine.py`, not against the modal's shorter captions. The engine's two bars are `base_complete` (the game's DEFAULT trophy group at 100%, which is the platinum on a plat game and the main list on one without) and `full_complete` (the whole game at 100%, DLC included), and those are beats 2 and 4. "Platinum every game" alone leaves a reader holding a non-plat game unable to tell whether it counts. {% endcomment %}
  1. 01

    Pick a set

    A franchise you grew up with, a studio you trust, or a collection someone curated. Every badge is one of these, and the whole catalogue is browsable before you commit.

  2. 02

    Earn the platinum in each

    Earn the platinum in every game in the set. A game with no platinum counts the moment its base trophy list hits 100%, so nothing in a set is unwinnable.

Every badge is drawn by hand

A small team of artists draws each one from scratch. Nothing in the catalogue is AI generated, and nothing ever will be.

{% comment %} EDITIONS, shown as OBJECTS on their own metals rather than listed in a table. The first edition rides the platinum plate and the rest the gold one, the same distinction the badge detail tabs and the Collection chips draw, so a reader meeting those later recognises the metal. Names and platforms come from `PlatformGroup` rows, never retyped: the model owns the mapping, the engine routes games by it, and its docstring calls adding a group "a row, not a schema change". The medallion is matched by INDEX, which is the honest limit of a two-metal illustration: a third seeded edition still gets a correct name, platforms and card, just the second metal. {% endcomment %}
