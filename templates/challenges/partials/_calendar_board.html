ONE LENS: A DAY IS FILLED BY A SHOVELWARE-FREE PLATINUM (owner, 2026-10-04). There were three, with a `.pp-switch` between the crests and the board to move between them, and the collapse took the switcher, the per-cell `{lens: bool}` dict, the `:has()` reveal rules, the doubled per-lens figures and the lens-blind screen-reader text with it. The owner's reasoning is the right distinction: "contracts fuel the other challenges but those are more curated sets of games and this is more wholistic". A Contract is a curated subset of the catalogue; a Calendar is a hunter's whole platinum history laid against the year. WHY A SWITCHER AND NOT A WALL OF TWELVE MONTHS (owner, 2026-10-04, choosing between three mockups). A whole year at 375px is tiny: the Hall of Fame hero draws exactly that and its cells land under 5px, which is a density read rather than something you can point at. One month at a time gives a 44px cell -- the touch minimum -- and it gives the twelve crests a job beyond decoration: they ARE the tabs. The year-at-a-glance read is not lost, it lives on the hero where it belongs. A DAY HAS NO WEEKDAY, which is why this grid has no `S M T W T F S` head and must never grow one. A square is a (month, day) pair with NO year -- "3 March" means every 3 March in the hunter's history -- so any weekday alignment would be a different lie every year. `calendar_render`'s module docstring states it at the source. The grid is a plain numbered one. ALL TWELVE PANELS ARE IN THE DOM. The rows are already fetched (one query for the whole board), so rendering everything and revealing with `hidden` makes the switch instant and request-free. NO COVER ART ANYWHERE (owner): "I don't think we do cover art for the calendar. Instead, we can let the user click on a day and it'll pop-up a modal that shows them games that satisfy that day." The modal is a separate slice; until it lands a day is not interactive, so it is a `

{% comment %}THE CREST ROW. Twelve tabs, and the run's trophy shelf. A SCROLLING SNAP STRIP AT MOBILE, twelve across from `md:`. Twelve tabs inside 375px is 22px each -- half the 44px touch minimum, and far too small for a face to say anything -- so the strip keeps each crest at a real size and lets the row scroll instead. SCROLLING IS THE EXCEPTION, NOT A HOUSE RULE. CLAUDE.md's mobile criterion says to OMIT rather than scroll, and the nearest precedent, `.nc__filter`, opens "IT WRAPS. It must not scroll". What makes this different is that the twelve are the board's NAVIGATION: omitting any puts a month out of reach, and wrapping to two rows of six stacks a second row of chrome over a board already behind a switcher. THE FACE IS THE MONTH'S ABBREVIATION, NOT A ROMAN NUMERAL, and this departs from the plan on purpose so it can be argued rather than discovered. The plan specified a numeral, settled before the layout was -- and the layout turned these coins into the SWITCHER. A switcher's first job is identification, and there is room for exactly three glyphs: the face is 12px, the project's type floor. `AUG` says the month outright; `VIII` is four glyphs and says it only to somebody already counting. The rim still carries position, which was the numeral's structural argument. THE CREST IS A BOOLEAN, AND ITS COLOUR IS THE MONTH. The crest used to be a metal -- bronze, silver or gold for the hardest of three lenses to complete the month -- which made it a cross-lens value on a page that switched lens, and cost two separate audit findings. One lens means one state, struck or not, which freed the colour axis for the twelve month hues the stylesheet now keys on `data-month`. That is why the hues are coherent here and were rejected when the crest was planned: a month hue and a lens metal on one coin were two colour systems competing for the same object.{% endcomment %}
