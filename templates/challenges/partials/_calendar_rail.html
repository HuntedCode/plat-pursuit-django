{% comment %}The day-marker rail: how many days are filled, and the next rung. A RAIL RATHER THAN A ROW OF AWARDS (owner, 2026-10-04, picking between three shapes). The day markers are the one part of this feature that moves from a hunter's very first platinum, so "here is your next rung" is the thing worth surfacing -- and a second row of medal-shaped objects would compete with the twelve crests rather than complement them. LINEAR IN DAYS, which is honest about the quantity and flattering about the climb. A day is the same distance everywhere along the rail because the rail measures days; the DIFFICULTY is not linear at all (the 50 rung is roughly 54 platinums, the 300 rung roughly 630), so the right-hand half is far harder than its spacing suggests. `DAY_MARKERS` carries the arithmetic. Distorting the spacing to show the difficulty would make the rail lie about the thing it measures, which is the worse of the two errors. NO REWARD NAMES, because there are none yet: the ladder's titles are a later slice. This draws the shape of the ladder and the hunter's place on it, and nothing that implies something is claimable. THE COMPARISON FIGURE LIVES HERE, which is the one place it has a job. `in_all` is the same days without the shovelware exclusion, and it is what makes the count mean something read aloud -- "297 days, of 340 you hold platinums for" -- rather than being a bare number. It is deliberately quiet: a reader who does not care never has to parse it. NO HEADLINE FIGURE OF ITS OWN.
